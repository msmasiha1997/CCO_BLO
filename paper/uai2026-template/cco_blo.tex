\begin{abstract}
Modern AI systems must optimize performance while controlling the probability of rare but consequential failures induced by stochasticity (noise, corruptions, distribution shift, or uncertain dynamics). Chance-constrained optimization provides a direct formalism for this goal, but is computationally challenging due to non-smooth quantile constraints and poor scaling with dimension and sample size.
We present \emph{Augmented Hypergradient CCO} (AH-CCO), a bilevel approach that reduces the chance constraint to a scalar inner problem via a superquantile variational characterization, then differentiates through the inner argmin using hypergradients. We combine a smooth strongly-convex surrogate for the inner objective with an augmented-Lagrangian-style penalty to obtain an end-to-end differentiable outer objective suitable for large-scale gradient methods.
We provide conditions under which the solution map is differentiable, derive an efficient gradient formula, and discuss approximation and optimization trade-offs. Experiments on standard synthetic benchmarks compare our method to TACO and conformal prediction based baselines and evaluate objective quality, reliability (empirical coverage), and runtime, including an additional coverage F-score metric.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
Many modern systems are deployed in regimes where \emph{average} performance is not the only objective: a small probability of failure under randomness, noise, corruptions, or distribution shift can dominate real-world risk.
Chance-constrained optimization (CCO) provides a direct formalism for designing such reliability-aware solutions and has long been studied in stochastic programming \citep{prekopa1995stochastic,shapiro2009lectures}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-cco}
  \min_{x\in\Xc} f(x)
  \quad\text{s.t.}\quad
  \mathbb{P}\!\left(g(x,Z)\le 0\right)\ge 1-\delta.
\end{equation}
Here $x\in\mathbb{R}^d$ is the decision variable constrained to a feasible set $\Xc$, $f:\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}$ is the objective, and $g:\mathbb{R}^d\times\mathbb{R}^m\to\mathbb{R}$ is a (possibly nonconvex) constraint function evaluated under a random vector $Z\sim P_Z$.
The safety level $\delta\in(0,1)$ controls the allowed violation probability.

Despite its conceptual clarity, CCO remains challenging at scale.
The feasibility event in \eqref{eq:intro-cco} is governed by a \emph{quantile} of the random variable $g(x,Z)$, which makes the constraint non-smooth and typically non-convex in $x$.
As a result, classical sample-based encodings can become computationally prohibitive as the decision dimension and sample size grow.
Recent work has proposed principled alternatives, including conservative CVaR surrogates \citep{rockafellaruryasev2000cvar}, a bilevel convex-optimization viewpoint and bundle-based algorithms \citep{laguelmalickackooij2021bilevel}, and solver-based conformal encodings with statistical guarantees \citep{zhao2024conformal}.
However, these approaches often trade scalability, objective quality, and reliability in different ways.

We propose \emph{Augmented Hypergradient CCO} (AH-CCO), a bilevel hypergradient method designed for high-dimensional CCO with sample access.
Our starting point is a variational characterization that introduces a \emph{scalar} inner variable representing a quantile/superquantile threshold.
We then (i) smooth this inner objective to obtain a unique inner minimizer and a differentiable solution map, and (ii) differentiate through the inner argmin using implicit hypergradients, following the modern bilevel-optimization toolkit \citep{pedregosa2016hyperparameter,franceschi2018bilevel,ji2021bilevel}.
Finally, we couple the resulting differentiable surrogate with an augmented-Lagrangian-style penalty, yielding an end-to-end objective amenable to stochastic gradient methods even when the inner problem is solved only approximately (as in practical hypergradient methods \citep{chen2024finding}).
Concretely, each iteration performs a warm-started one-dimensional bisection solve for the inner variable (Algorithm~\ref{alg:inner-bisect}) and then takes an outer gradient step in $x$ using the resulting implicit hypergradient (Algorithm~\ref{alg:ahcco}).

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Algorithm.} We introduce AH-CCO, a two-loop stochastic bilevel method: an inner one-dimensional solve (bisection) computes a smoothed quantile proxy and an outer gradient step updates $x$ using an implicit hypergradient, with an augmented penalty to enforce feasibility.
  \item \textbf{Theory.} We provide sufficient conditions for differentiability of the inner solution map, derive an efficient gradient expression, and analyze the effect of inexact inner solves and minibatch sampling on the outer optimization.
  \item \textbf{Experiments.} We provide a unified benchmark across diverse CCO baselines---including TACO \citep{laguelmalickackooij2021bilevel}, conformal predictive programming solvers \citep{zhao2024conformal}, and CVaR-penalty surrogates \citep{rockafellaruryasev2000cvar}---and study dimension/sample/safety scaling on synthetic tasks using reliability-aware metrics.
  We also evaluate a data-driven robustness application on ImageNet-100 under common corruptions \citep{deng2009imagenet,hendrycks2019benchmarking}, comparing against practical baselines such as AugMix, TRADES, CVaR, and GroupDRO \citep{hendrycks2020augmix,zhang2019theoretically,rockafellaruryasev2000cvar,sagawa2020distributionally}.
\end{itemize}

Empirically, AH-CCO achieves consistently high reliability across dimension/sample/safety sweeps on synthetic CCO benchmarks while remaining computationally lightweight compared to solver-based conformal encodings.
On ImageNet-100 corruption robustness, AH-CCO improves the joint accuracy--reliability profile relative to widely-used practical baselines (Table~\ref{tab:imagenet100-main}, Figures~\ref{fig:imagenet100-acc}--\ref{fig:imagenet100-fec}).

\section{Problem Setup}\label{sec:setup}
\subsection{Chance-Constrained Optimization}
Let $\Xc\subseteq \mathbb{R}^d$ be a nonempty closed convex set, let $Z\sim P_Z$ be a random vector on $\mathbb{R}^m$, let $f:\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}$ be an objective, and let $g:\mathbb{R}^d\times\mathbb{R}^m\to\mathbb{R}$ be a (random) constraint function.
We study the chance-constrained optimization (CCO) problem
\begin{align}
  \min_{x\in \Xc}\; & f(x)\label{eq:cco}\\
  \text{s.t.}\; & \mathbb{P}\big(g(x,Z)\le 0\big)\ge 1-\delta,\nonumber
\end{align}
where $\delta\in(0,1)$ controls the maximum acceptable probability of violation.
Equivalently, the violation probability satisfies $\mathbb{P}(g(x,Z)>0)\le \delta$.

The chance constraint is governed by a quantile of the random variable $g(x,Z)$.
As a result, the feasible set
\[
  \{x\in\Xc:\mathbb{P}(g(x,Z)\le 0)\ge 1-\delta\}
\]
is generally \emph{not} convex, even when $f$ and $g(\cdot,z)$ are convex.
Moreover, naive sample-based encodings are combinatorial and scale poorly with dimension and sample size.
Sections~\ref{sec:background}--\ref{sec:method} address these issues by (i) reformulating the chance constraint as a scalar bilevel problem via quantiles/superquantiles and (ii) introducing a smooth, strongly convex surrogate that enables implicit differentiation and scalable stochastic optimization.

\subsection{Access Model and Sampling}
We do not assume closed-form access to $P_Z$.
Instead, we assume access to i.i.d.\ samples $z^{(1)},\dots,z^{(M)}\sim P_Z$, which is the standard setting in data-driven optimization and learning.

All methods we consider ultimately operate on finite samples, either by replacing expectations with sample averages or by solving a deterministic encoding of the sampled chance constraint.
To enable meaningful comparisons and out-of-sample evaluation, we explicitly separate \emph{training samples} (used by the algorithms) from \emph{evaluation samples} (used only for reporting).
The concrete experimental protocol (including how we draw and reuse training/evaluation samples for fair comparisons) is described in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

\paragraph{Empirical Coverage (Reliability Metric)}
For a candidate solution $x$, we measure reliability via the \emph{empirical coverage} computed on fresh i.i.d.\ samples $\tilde z^{(1)},\dots,\tilde z^{(N)}\sim P_Z$:
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\mathrm{EC}}_N(x)
  \;:=\;
  \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \mathbf{1}\!\left\{g\!\left(x,\tilde z^{(i)}\right)\le 0\right\}.\label{eq:ec}
\end{equation}
The chance constraint in \eqref{eq:cco} targets $\mathrm{EC}(x)=\mathbb{P}(g(x,Z)\le 0)\ge 1-\delta$, while $\widehat{\mathrm{EC}}_N(x)$ provides an unbiased Monte Carlo estimate of $\mathrm{EC}(x)$.

\begin{assumption}[Regularity of $f$, $g$, and $P_Z$]\label{ass:setup}
We assume:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item (\textbf{Objective}) $f$ is convex and differentiable on $\Xc$ and $\nabla f(x)$ is available.
  \item (\textbf{Constraint}) For $P_Z$-almost every $z$, $x\mapsto g(x,z)$ is convex and differentiable on $\Xc$ and $\nabla_x g(x,z)$ is available.
  \item (\textbf{Sampling}) We can draw i.i.d.\ samples $z^{(1)},\dots,z^{(M)}\sim P_Z$.
  \item (\textbf{Integrability}) For each $x\in\Xc$, $g(x,Z)$ and $\nabla_x g(x,Z)$ are integrable.
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

\paragraph{Remark on extensions.}
For clarity, we focus on a single chance constraint in \eqref{eq:cco}; extensions to multiple constraints are discussed in Section~\ref{sec:method}.

\subsection{Quantiles and Superquantiles}\label{sec:background}
The chance constraint in \eqref{eq:cco} is governed by a \emph{quantile} of the scalar random variable $g(x,Z)$.
Making this explicit lets us replace a probability constraint with an equivalent scalar inequality.

Quantiles are one-dimensional objects.
This turns feasibility into controlling a single scalar summary of the distribution of $g(x,Z)$, which is the starting point for scalable algorithms that avoid combinatorial sample encodings.

We first rewrite the chance constraint as a quantile constraint.
We then introduce a superquantile (also known as conditional value-at-risk) variational representation that introduces an auxiliary scalar variable.
This yields a simple bilevel viewpoint: the outer decision $x$ is optimized while an inner scalar is chosen to summarize the tail of $g(x,Z)$.
Finally, we explain why this bilevel map can be \emph{unique but nonsmooth}, motivating the smooth, strongly convex surrogate in Section~\ref{sec:method}.

\subsubsection{Quantile reformulation}
We begin by expressing the chance constraint via a quantile.
For a scalar random variable $Y$ with CDF $F_Y(t):=\mathbb{P}(Y\le t)$, define its $p$-quantile (value-at-risk) for any $p\in(0,1)$ as
\begin{equation}
  \Q_{p}(Y)\;:=\;\inf\{t\in\mathbb{R}: F_Y(t)\ge p\}.\label{eq:quantile-def}
\end{equation}
In particular, with $p=1-\delta$ and $Y=g(x,Z)$, the chance constraint in \eqref{eq:cco} is equivalent to
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{P}\big(g(x,Z)\le 0\big)\ge 1-\delta
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \Q_{1-\delta}\big(g(x,Z)\big)\le 0.\label{eq:quantile}
\end{equation}
Thus, feasibility is controlled by a single scalar summary of the distribution of $g(x,Z)$.

\subsubsection{Superquantiles and a scalar inner problem}
The \emph{superquantile} (conditional value-at-risk) at level $1-\delta$ can be defined as the average of upper quantiles,
\begin{equation}
  \SQ_{1-\delta}(Y)\;:=\;\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{1-\delta}^1 \Q_{u}(Y)\,du.\label{eq:sq-def}
\end{equation}
When $Y$ has a continuous distribution, this agrees with the more familiar conditional-expectation form
$\SQ_{1-\delta}(Y)=\mathbb{E}[Y\mid Y\ge \Q_{1-\delta}(Y)]$.

The superquantile admits a convex variational representation with an auxiliary scalar variable.
This makes it a natural bridge between quantiles (chance constraints) and optimization.

A key fact is that superquantiles admit a variational representation as the minimum of a convex function in a scalar auxiliary variable $s$ \citep{rockafellaruryasev2000cvar}:
\begin{equation}
  \SQ_{1-\delta}(Y)=\min_{s\in\mathbb{R}} \left\{ s + \frac{1}{\delta}\,\mathbb{E}\big[(Y-s)_+\big] \right\},\label{eq:cvar-var}
\end{equation}
where $(u)_+:=\max\{u,0\}$.
The minimizer set in \eqref{eq:cvar-var} is closely related to the quantile $\Q_{1-\delta}(Y)$; in particular, if the CDF of $Y$ is strictly increasing at $q=\Q_{1-\delta}(Y)$ (e.g., $Y$ admits a density that is strictly positive in a neighborhood of $q$), then the minimizer is unique and equals $q$ \citep{rockafellaruryasev2000cvar}.

Specializing to $Y=g(x,Z)$ motivates the scalar inner objective
\begin{equation}
  G_\delta(x,s)\;:=\; s + \frac{1}{\delta}\,\mathbb{E}_Z\!\left[(g(x,Z)-s)_+\right].\label{eq:G}
\end{equation}
Define the associated solution map
\begin{equation}
  s^\star(x)\;\in\;\arg\min_{s\in\mathbb{R}} G_\delta(x,s).\label{eq:sstar-def}
\end{equation}
When the minimizer is unique, we write $s^\star(x)$ for the unique scalar minimizer.
Under the uniqueness condition above, $s^\star(x)$ coincides with the $(1-\delta)$-quantile of $g(x,Z)$, hence the chance constraint \eqref{eq:quantile} is equivalent to $s^\star(x)\le 0$.
This yields the bilevel viewpoint of CCO developed in \citet{laguelmalickackooij2021bilevel}.

\begin{lemma}[Uniqueness of the inner minimizer]\label{lem:unique-sstar}
Fix $x\in\Xc$ and define $Y:=g(x,Z)$.
Assume that $P_Z$ is absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb{R}^{m}$ (denoted $P_Z\ll \mathrm{Leb}$) and admits a continuous density, and that $z\mapsto g(x,z)$ is continuous.
Assume moreover that the induced scalar random variable $Y$ has a continuous distribution and that its CDF is strictly increasing at $q:=\Q_{1-\delta}(Y)$.
Then the scalar inner problem \eqref{eq:sstar-def} has a unique minimizer, and the unique minimizer satisfies
\[
  s^\star(x)=q=\Q_{1-\delta}(Y)=\Q_{1-\delta}(g(x,Z)).
\]
\end{lemma}
\emph{Proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.}

\subsubsection{Uniqueness vs.\ differentiability of the quantile map}

From \eqref{eq:G}--\eqref{eq:sstar-def}, the chance constraint can be written as $s^\star(x)\le 0$ where
$s^\star(x)\in\arg\min_{s\in\mathbb{R}} G_\delta(x,s)$ and $G_\delta(x,s)=s+\frac{1}{\delta}\mathbb{E}[(g(x,Z)-s)_+]$.
To optimize over $x$, it is natural to ask whether the map $x\mapsto s^\star(x)$ admits a usable derivative.

\paragraph{What can go wrong even when $s^\star(x)$ is unique?}
For each fixed $x$, the function $s\mapsto G_\delta(x,s)$ is convex.
If $Y=g(x,Z)$ has no point mass, then $G_\delta(x,s)$ is differentiable in $s$ and the optimality condition for $s^\star(x)$ can be written as
\[
  \partial_s G_\delta(x,s^\star(x))=0,
  \qquad
  \partial_s G_\delta(x,s)=1-\frac{1}{\delta}\mathbb{P}\big(g(x,Z)>s\big).
\]
When the $(1-\delta)$-quantile is unique (Lemma~\ref{lem:unique-sstar}), this condition identifies a unique $s^\star(x)$.
However, the dependence of $s^\star(x)$ on $x$ can still fail to be differentiable.

\paragraph{Why is differentiability nontrivial?}
The positive-part function introduces kinks.
In particular, under the same ``no point mass'' condition, differentiating $G_\delta$ with respect to $x$ brings in an indicator:
\[
  \nabla_x G_\delta(x,s)
  =
  \frac{1}{\delta}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathbf{1}\!\left\{g(x,Z)>s\right\}\nabla_x g(x,Z)\right].
\]
As $x$ changes, the event $\{g(x,Z)>s\}$ can change abruptly (points move in and out of the tail), which can create kinks in $x\mapsto s^\star(x)$ even if $s^\star(x)$ is unique.
\paragraph{Example.}
Let $m=1$, let $Z\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,1]$, and define $g(x,z):=|x|-z$.
Then $g(x,Z)$ has a continuous distribution and $\Q_{1-\delta}(g(x,Z))=|x|-\delta$, so $s^\star(x)=|x|-\delta$ is unique but not differentiable at $x=0$.

\paragraph{How do we address it?}
Direct implicit differentiation of the argmin map typically requires twice differentiability and strong convexity in the inner variable \citep{gould2016argmin,pedregosa2016hyperparameter}.
Section~\ref{sec:method} addresses this by replacing $(\cdot)_+$ with a $C^2$ smoothing and adding a small strongly convex regularizer in $s$, making the inner minimizer differentiable in $x$.

\section{Method: Augmented Hypergradient CCO}\label{sec:method}
We now describe \emph{Augmented Hypergradient CCO} (AH-CCO), our bilevel augmented approach.
We begin by deriving the bilevel reformulation of CCO and then present a scalable hypergradient-based algorithm for solving it.

\subsection{From chance feasibility to a bilevel constraint}
Equation~\eqref{eq:sstar-def} defines the scalar inner solution map $s^\star(x)\in\arg\min_s G_\delta(x,s)$.
Under the uniqueness condition in Lemma~\ref{lem:unique-sstar}, $s^\star(x)$ coincides with the $(1-\delta)$-quantile of $g(x,Z)$, so the chance constraint in \eqref{eq:cco} is equivalent to $s^\star(x)\le 0$.
Substituting this characterization into \eqref{eq:cco} yields the bilevel constrained reformulation
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \min_{x\in\Xc}\; & f(x)\\
    \text{s.t.}\; & s^\star(x)\le 0,\\
    & s^\star(x)=\arg\min_{s\in\mathbb{R}} G_\delta(x,s).
  \end{aligned}\label{eq:outer-constrained}
\end{equation}
Our goal is to optimize $f(x)$ while driving the scalar quantile $s^\star(x)$ below zero.
The main challenge is that $s^\star(x)$ can be non-differentiable in $x$ (Section~\ref{sec:background}), which obstructs direct gradient-based optimization.
We address this by (i) smoothing and strongly convexifying the inner problem to obtain a differentiable surrogate $\widehat s^\star(x)$, and (ii) penalizing constraint violation through a differentiable augmented objective.

\subsection{A differentiable augmented objective}
We enforce $s^\star(x)\le 0$ via a standard Lagrangian relaxation. The constrained problem \eqref{eq:outer-constrained} can be written as
\begin{equation}
  \min_{x\in\Xc}\;\max_{\lambda\ge 0}\; f(x) + \lambda s^\star(x).\label{eq:minmax}
\end{equation}
The max in \eqref{eq:minmax} is non-smooth in $x$.
We therefore introduce a quadratic regularization on the dual variable (an augmented-Lagrangian-style smoothing):
\begin{equation}
  \min_{x\in\Xc}\;\max_{\lambda\ge 0}\; f(x) + \lambda s^\star(x) - \frac{\mu}{2}\lambda^2,\label{eq:aug-lag}
\end{equation}
where $\mu>0$ controls the strength of the regularization.
Maximizing over $\lambda$ yields the equivalent penalized objective
\begin{equation}
  F_\mu(x)\;:=\; f(x) + \frac{([s^\star(x)]_+)^2}{2\mu}.\label{eq:Fmu}
\end{equation}
When $s^\star(x)\le 0$, the penalty term vanishes; when $s^\star(x)>0$, it grows quadratically.

\subsection{Relaxing the inner problem: smoothness and strong convexity}\label{sec:inner-relax}
To optimize \eqref{eq:Fmu} with gradients, we need to differentiate through the map $x\mapsto s^\star(x)$.
As discussed in Section~\ref{sec:background}, $s^\star(x)$ is generally not differentiable because $(u)_+$ is not $C^2$.
We therefore replace the hinge with a $C^2$ smoothing and add a strongly convex term in $s$.

\paragraph{Smooth approximation of the hinge.}
We use a twice continuously differentiable approximation $h_\theta:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ to $(\cdot)_+$ with smoothing parameter $\theta>0$:
\begin{equation}
  h_\theta(u)=
  \begin{cases}
    -\dfrac{u^4}{16\theta^3} + \dfrac{3u^2}{8\theta} + \dfrac{u}{2} + \dfrac{3\theta}{16}, & |u|\le \theta,\\[6pt]
    \max\{u,0\}, & \text{otherwise.}
  \end{cases}\label{eq:htheta}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Strongly convex surrogate.}
Define the surrogate inner objective
\begin{equation}
  \widehat G_{\delta,\theta,\varepsilon}(x,s)
  \;:=\;
  s + \frac{1}{\delta}\,\mathbb{E}_Z\!\left[h_\theta\!\left(g(x,Z)-s\right)\right]
  + \frac{\varepsilon}{2}s^2,\label{eq:Ghat}
\end{equation}
with $\varepsilon>0$.
For each $x$, the function $s\mapsto \widehat G_{\delta,\theta,\varepsilon}(x,s)$ is $\varepsilon$-strongly convex and $C^2$, hence it has a unique minimizer
\begin{equation}
  \widehat s^\star(x)\;:=\;\arg\min_{s\in\mathbb{R}} \widehat G_{\delta,\theta,\varepsilon}(x,s).\label{eq:shat-star}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Hypergradients via implicit differentiation}
Because the inner problem \eqref{eq:shat-star} is strongly convex and smooth in $s$, the solution map $x\mapsto \widehat s^\star(x)$ is differentiable under mild regularity assumptions (e.g., $g(\cdot,z)$ is differentiable for almost every $z$ and derivatives can be exchanged with expectations).
Let $\phi(x,s):=\partial_s \widehat G_{\delta,\theta,\varepsilon}(x,s)$.
The optimality condition $\phi(x,\widehat s^\star(x))=0$ and the fact that $\partial_s\phi(x,s)=\partial^2_{ss}\widehat G_{\delta,\theta,\varepsilon}(x,s)\ge \varepsilon$ imply (by the implicit function theorem) that $\widehat s^\star(x)$ is $C^1$ and
\begin{equation}
  \nabla_x \widehat s^\star(x)
  \;=\;
  - \frac{\partial^2_{xs}\widehat G_{\delta,\theta,\varepsilon}(x,s)}{\partial^2_{ss}\widehat G_{\delta,\theta,\varepsilon}(x,s)}
  \bigg|_{s=\widehat s^\star(x)}.\label{eq:hypergrad}
\end{equation}
We then optimize the differentiable surrogate of \eqref{eq:Fmu}:
\begin{equation}
  \widehat F_\mu(x)\;:=\; f(x) + \frac{([\widehat s^\star(x)]_+)^2}{2\mu}.\label{eq:Fhat}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Sample-based implementation}
In practice, we approximate expectations by sample averages.
At each outer iteration $k$, we draw an i.i.d.\ minibatch $\{z_k^{(j)}\}_{j=1}^M\sim P_Z$ and reuse it for (i) the inner solve for $s$ and (ii) the hypergradient computation in \eqref{eq:hypergrad}.
For readability, we suppress the iteration index and simply write $\{z^{(j)}\}_{j=1}^M$.
Denote this minibatch by $B:=\{z^{(j)}\}_{j=1}^M$.
We then use the plug-in estimator
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \widehat G_{\delta,\theta,\varepsilon}(x,s;B)
  \;:=\;
  &\, s + \frac{1}{\delta M}\sum_{j=1}^M h_\theta\!\bigl(g(x,z^{(j)})-s\bigr)\\
  &\;+\; \frac{\varepsilon}{2}s^2,
  \end{aligned}\label{eq:Ghat-plugin}
\end{equation}
Let $\widehat s_M^\star(x):=\arg\min_{s\in\mathbb{R}}\widehat G_{\delta,\theta,\varepsilon}(x,s;B)$ denote the sample-based inner minimizer.
It can be obtained efficiently because the problem is one-dimensional.
Moreover, the derivative
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \MoveEqLeft[2]\widehat\phi_M(x,s)
  \;:=\;
  \partial_s \widehat G_{\delta,\theta,\varepsilon}(x,s;B)\\
  &\;=\;
  \,1-\frac{1}{\delta M}\sum_{j=1}^M h'_\theta(g(x,z^{(j)})-s)\;+\;\varepsilon s
  \end{aligned}\label{eq:phi-plugin}
\end{equation}
is strictly increasing in $s$ (by $\varepsilon$-strong convexity), so $\widehat s_M^\star(x)$ is the unique root of $\widehat\phi_M(x,s)=0$.
Moreover, since $0\le h'_\theta\le 1$, we have $\widehat\phi_M(x,-1/\varepsilon)\le 0$ and $\widehat\phi_M(x,1/(\delta\varepsilon))\ge 1$, hence $\widehat s_M^\star(x)\in[-1/\varepsilon,\;1/(\delta\varepsilon)]$ for all $x$ and minibatches.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:inner-bisect} gives a robust warm-started bisection procedure for this inner solve.
In the outer loop, we use $\widehat s_M^\star(x)$ (and its derivatives) in place of $\widehat s^\star(x)$ to form a stochastic gradient of $\widehat F_\mu(x)$, optionally projecting back onto $\Xc$.
We may decrease $\mu$ over iterations to sharpen feasibility while keeping gradients stable.

\paragraph{Main algorithm.}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:ahcco} summarizes AH-CCO.
Each outer iteration draws a fresh minibatch $B\sim P_Z$, warm-starts the one-dimensional inner solve for $\widehat s_M^\star(x_k)$ using $s_k$ (Algorithm~\ref{alg:inner-bisect}), computes the corresponding hypergradient via \eqref{eq:hypergrad} using the same minibatch, and then performs a stochastic gradient step on the penalized objective $\widehat F_{\mu_k}$ in \eqref{eq:Fhat}.
The penalty parameter $\mu_k$ can be decreased over time to progressively tighten feasibility.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
  \caption{AH-CCO: Augmented Hypergradient CCO}\label{alg:ahcco}
  \begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \Require safety level $\delta$, smoothing $\theta$, strong convexity $\varepsilon$, penalty schedule $\{\mu_k\}_{k=0}^{K-1}$, batch size $M$, initial $x_0\in\Xc$
    \State initialize $s_0\in\mathbb{R}$
    \For{$k=0,1,\dots,K-1$}
      \State sample minibatch $B=\{z^{(j)}\}_{j=1}^M\sim P_Z$
      \State \textbf{(inner)} $s_{k+1}\gets$ Algorithm~\ref{alg:inner-bisect}$(x_k,s_k,B,\delta,\theta,\varepsilon)$
      \State \textbf{(hypergradient)} compute $\nabla_x \widehat s_M^\star(x_k)$ via \eqref{eq:hypergrad} using the same minibatch
      \State \textbf{(outer)} form $\nabla_x \widehat F_{\mu_k}(x_k)$ for $\widehat F_{\mu}(x)=f(x)+\frac{([\widehat s_M^\star(x)]_+)^2}{2\mu}$
      \State update $x_{k+1}\leftarrow \Pi_{\Xc}\!\left(x_k - \eta_k \nabla_x \widehat F_{\mu_k}(x_k)\right)$ (or use Adam)
    \EndFor
    \State \Return $x_K$
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
  \caption{Inner-loop bisection for $\widehat s_M^\star(x)$}\label{alg:inner-bisect}
  \begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \Require $x$, warm start $s^{(0)}$, minibatch $B=\{z^{(j)}\}_{j=1}^M$, $(\delta,\theta,\varepsilon)$, tolerance $\tau>0$, max iters $T$
    \State initialize bracket $[s_L,s_U]\gets[-1/\varepsilon,\;1/(\delta\varepsilon)]$ \hfill (guaranteed by $0\le h'_\theta\le 1$ and \eqref{eq:phi-plugin})
    \If{$s^{(0)}\in[s_L,s_U]$} \hfill (optional warm start)
      \If{$\widehat\phi_M(x,s^{(0)})\le 0$} \State $s_L\gets s^{(0)}$ \Else \State $s_U\gets s^{(0)}$ \EndIf
    \EndIf
    \For{$t=1,2,\dots,T$}
      \State $s_M \gets (s_L+s_U)/2$
      \If{$\widehat\phi_M(x,s_M)\le 0$} \State $s_L \gets s_M$ \Else \State $s_U \gets s_M$ \EndIf
      \If{$|s_U-s_L|\le \tau$} \State \textbf{break} \EndIf
    \EndFor
    \State \Return $(s_L+s_U)/2$
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Theory}\label{sec:theory}
The goal of this section is to connect the differentiable surrogate optimized by AH-CCO back to the original chance constraint and to justify the use of stochastic hypergradient updates.
Recall that the chance constraint in \eqref{eq:cco} is equivalent to the quantile inequality
\[
  q(x):=\Q_{1-\delta}(g(x,Z))\le 0.
\]
Section~\ref{sec:theory-approx} quantifies how the relaxed inner minimizer $\widehat s^\star(x)$ approximates $q(x)$ as $\theta,\varepsilon\to 0$, implying that the surrogate feasibility set $\{x:\widehat s^\star(x)\le 0\}$ approximates the true feasible set $\{x:q(x)\le 0\}$ up to a controllable slack.
Section~\ref{sec:theory-sgd} then provides a standard stationarity guarantee for stochastic gradient updates applied to the smooth surrogate outer objective $\widehat F_\mu$, explicitly accounting for minibatch estimation and inexact inner solves.

These results clarify (i) what is lost by smoothing/regularization and (ii) why the resulting objective can be optimized efficiently with first-order methods.

We decompose the approximation error into a \emph{smoothing bias} term ($O(\theta)$) and a \emph{regularization bias} term ($O(\varepsilon)$) under a standard local density condition at the target quantile.

\subsection{Approximation of the quantile map}\label{sec:theory-approx}
Fix $x\in\Xc$ and define the scalar random variable $Y:=g(x,Z)$, with quantile $q:=\Q_{1-\delta}(Y)$.
Recall that the relaxed inner objective is
\[
  \widehat G_{\delta,\theta,\varepsilon}(x,s)
  =
  s + \frac{1}{\delta}\mathbb{E}\!\left[h_\theta(Y-s)\right] + \frac{\varepsilon}{2}s^2,
\]
and its unique minimizer is $\widehat s^\star(x)=\arg\min_s \widehat G_{\delta,\theta,\varepsilon}(x,s)$ (uniqueness follows from $\varepsilon$-strong convexity in $s$).
We relate $\widehat s^\star(x)$ to the original quantile $q$.

\begin{assumption}[Local regularity at the target quantile]\label{ass:quantile-regularity}
Fix $x\in\Xc$, define $Y:=g(x,Z)$, and let $q:=\Q_{1-\delta}(Y)$.
Assume that $Y$ admits a density $p_Y$ on a neighborhood of $q$, and that $p_Y$ is continuous at $q$ with $p_Y(q)>0$ (i.e., $\lim_{t\to q}p_Y(t)=p_Y(q)$).
By continuity and $p_Y(q)>0$, there exist constants $m>0$ and $r>0$ such that
\begin{equation}
  p_Y(t)\ge m\qquad\text{for all }t\in[q-r,q+r].\label{eq:density-lower}
\end{equation}
\end{assumption}

\paragraph{Discussion.}
Condition \eqref{eq:density-lower} is the usual \emph{density bounded away from zero at the quantile} assumption.
It rules out flat CDF regions at the target level (equivalently, $F_Y$ is strictly increasing at $q$), and it implies that the quantile functional is locally stable: small perturbations of tail probability translate to $O(1/m)$ changes in $q$ \citep{vandervaart1998asymptotic,koenker2005quantile}.
Such a local lower bound holds under mild smoothness/nondegeneracy conditions on the pushforward map $z\mapsto g(x,z)$ when $Z$ has a smooth density; it is satisfied by our synthetic setups.
It holds for our synthetic problems (Gaussian and exponential tails).
We defer two technical lemmas used in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:quantile-approx} to Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.


We next quantify how the relaxed inner minimizer $\widehat s^\star(x)$ approximates the true quantile $q$.
The approximation error has two sources: (i) replacing the hinge with its $C^2$ smoothing $h_\theta$ introduces an $O(\theta)$ bias, and (ii) adding the strongly convex term $\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}s^2$ makes the inner problem differentiable but induces an additional $O(\varepsilon)$ shift.
The constant in the $O(\varepsilon)$ term depends on how much probability mass lies near the quantile, captured by the density lower bound \eqref{eq:density-lower}.

\begin{theorem}[Quantile approximation under $\theta$-smoothing and $\varepsilon$-regularization]\label{thm:quantile-approx}
Let $q=\Q_{1-\delta}(Y)$ and assume \eqref{eq:density-lower} holds.
Fix $\theta\le r/2$ and let $\widehat s^\star$ be the unique minimizer of $\widehat G_{\delta,\theta,\varepsilon}(x,\cdot)$ for some $\varepsilon>0$.
Assume the regularization is small enough that
\begin{equation}
  \delta\varepsilon\left(|q|+\frac{r}{2}\right)\;<\; m\left(\frac{r}{2}-\theta\right).\label{eq:eps-localize}
\end{equation}
Then $\widehat s^\star\in[q-r/2,q+r/2]$ and
\begin{equation}
  |\widehat s^\star - q|
  \;\le\;
  \theta + \frac{16\delta}{9m}\left(|q|+\frac{r}{2}\right)\varepsilon.\label{eq:shat-q-bound}
\end{equation}
In particular, if $\theta\to 0$ and $\varepsilon\to 0$ then $\widehat s^\star\to q$.
\end{theorem}
The proof is given in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.
The localization condition \eqref{eq:eps-localize} ensures that the regularization does not push $\widehat s^\star$ outside the neighborhood where the density lower bound \eqref{eq:density-lower} holds, which is needed for an explicit bound with $1/m$ scaling.

\paragraph{Implication for feasibility sets.}
The original chance constraint is equivalent to $q(x)\le 0$.
Let $\mathcal{F}:=\{x\in\Xc:q(x)\le 0\}$ be the true feasible set and let $\widehat{\mathcal{F}}:=\{x\in\Xc:\widehat s^\star(x)\le 0\}$ be the surrogate feasible set.
For each $x$, define the pointwise tolerance $e(x)$ as the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:shat-q-bound} (with the corresponding constants $m,r$ for that $x$).
Then Theorem~\ref{thm:quantile-approx} implies the sandwich relation
\[
  \begin{aligned}
    \{x\in\Xc:q(x)\le -e(x)\}
    &\subseteq \widehat{\mathcal{F}},\\
    \widehat{\mathcal{F}}
    &\subseteq \{x\in\Xc:q(x)\le e(x)\}.
  \end{aligned}
\]
Thus CCO-feasible points with a margin remain feasible for the surrogate, and surrogate feasibility implies CCO feasibility up to a quantile slack controlled by $(\theta,\varepsilon)$.

\paragraph{Bounding $|q(x)|$ on $\Xc$.}
If $\Xc$ has finite diameter $D_{\Xc}:=\sup_{x,y\in\Xc}\|x-y\|<\infty$ and $g(\cdot,z)$ is $G$-Lipschitz on $\Xc$ uniformly in $z$ (e.g., $\|\nabla_x g(x,z)\|\le G$ for all $x\in\Xc$ and $P_Z$-a.e.\ $z$), then $|g(x,Z)-g(x_0,Z)|\le GD_{\Xc}$ almost surely for any $x_0\in\Xc$.
In particular,
\[
  q(x_0)-GD_{\Xc}\ \le\ q(x)\ \le\ q(x_0)+GD_{\Xc},
\]
and therefore $|q(x)|\le |q(x_0)|+GD_{\Xc}$ for all $x\in\Xc$.
This type of bound can be combined with Theorem~\ref{thm:quantile-approx} (and uniform regularity constants) to obtain a uniform feasibility slack across $x$.


Define $H_\theta(s):=\mathbb{E}[h'_\theta(Y-s)]$.
The first-order optimality condition for $\widehat s^\star$ can be written as
\begin{equation}
  0 = 1 - \frac{1}{\delta}H_\theta(\widehat s^\star) + \varepsilon \widehat s^\star
  \Longleftrightarrow
  H_\theta(\widehat s^\star)=\delta(1+\varepsilon \widehat s^\star).\label{eq:Htheta-root}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:quantile-approx} provides a concrete bias decomposition: the smoothing error decays linearly in $\theta$, while the strong convexity term induces an additional bias that decays linearly in $\varepsilon$.
At the same time, $\varepsilon$ controls numerical stability of hypergradients through \eqref{eq:hypergrad} (via $(\partial^2_{ss}\widehat G)^{-1}\simeq 1/\varepsilon$), so $\varepsilon$ trades off stability against accuracy.
Condition \eqref{eq:eps-localize} formalizes the ``small enough'' regime in which this trade-off yields a clean, localized bound.

\subsection{Stochastic optimization with inexact inner solves}\label{sec:theory-sgd}
Section~\ref{sec:method} proposes to minimize the smooth surrogate
\[
  \widehat F_\mu(x)= f(x) + \frac{([\widehat s^\star(x)]_+)^2}{2\mu},
\]
where $\widehat s^\star(x)$ is defined by \eqref{eq:shat-star}.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:ahcco} is stochastic in two ways:
(i) expectations are replaced by minibatch averages, and
(ii) the inner minimizer $\widehat s^\star(x_k)$ is computed approximately and then plugged into the hypergradient formula \eqref{eq:hypergrad} and the outer objective \eqref{eq:Fhat}.
We state a convergence guarantee that explicitly accounts for these inexactness sources.

\begin{assumption}[Smoothness, sampling, and inexact inner solves]\label{ass:stoch}
Assume:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item (\textbf{Smoothness}) $f$ is differentiable, bounded below, and has $L_f$-Lipschitz gradient on $\Xc$.
  For $P_Z$-a.e.\ $z$, $x\mapsto g(x,z)$ is twice differentiable on $\Xc$ and there exist constants $G_g,H_g>0$ such that
  $\|\nabla_x g(x,z)\|\le G_g$ and $\|\nabla^2_{xx} g(x,z)\|\le H_g$ for all $x\in\Xc$.
  \item (\textbf{Sampling}) At each iteration $k$, we draw an i.i.d.\ minibatch $\mathcal{B}_k=\{z_k^{(j)}\}_{j=1}^M$ from $P_Z$, independently across $k$.
  \item (\textbf{Inner loop}) Let $\widehat G_{\delta,\theta,\varepsilon}(x,s;\mathcal{B}_k)$ denote the sample-average inner objective in \eqref{eq:Ghat-plugin}, and let
  $\widehat s^\star(x_k;\mathcal{B}_k):=\arg\min_s \widehat G_{\delta,\theta,\varepsilon}(x_k,s;\mathcal{B}_k)$ be its unique minimizer.
  The inner solver performs $T$ bisection steps on the first-order condition $\partial_s\widehat G_{\delta,\theta,\varepsilon}(x_k,s;\mathcal{B}_k)=0$, initialized with the bracket
  \begin{equation}
    s^{\mathrm{L}}:=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon},
    \qquad
    s^{\mathrm{U}}:=\frac{1}{\delta\varepsilon},\label{eq:inner-bracket}
  \end{equation}
  and returns an approximate solution $\tilde s_k\in[s^{\mathrm{L}},s^{\mathrm{U}}]$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

\begin{lemma}[Inner-loop accuracy]\label{lem:inner-accuracy}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:stoch}, the bisection inner loop satisfies
\begin{equation}
  |\tilde s_k-\widehat s^\star(x_k;\mathcal{B}_k)|
  \;\le\;
  \frac{s^{\mathrm{U}}-s^{\mathrm{L}}}{2^T}
  \;=\;
  \frac{1+\delta}{\delta\,\varepsilon}\,2^{-T}.\label{eq:inner-bisect-rate}
\end{equation}
Moreover, letting $\widehat s^\star(x_k)$ denote the population minimizer in \eqref{eq:shat-star}, we have
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{E}\!\left[|\tilde s_k-\widehat s^\star(x_k)|\mid x_k\right]
  \;\le\;
  \frac{1+\delta}{\delta\,\varepsilon}\,2^{-T}
  + \frac{1}{2\delta\,\varepsilon\sqrt{M}}
  \;=:\;\epsilon'.\label{eq:inner-eps-s}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\emph{Proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.}

\paragraph{Inexact stochastic gradients.}
Let $\widehat F_\mu^{\inf}:=\inf_{x\in\Xc}\widehat F_\mu(x)$.
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:stoch} and the smoothness of $h_\theta$, the map $x\mapsto \widehat s^\star(x)$ is continuously differentiable and $\widehat F_\mu$ has $L$-Lipschitz gradient on $\Xc$ for some $L$ that depends on $(L_f,G_g,H_g,\theta,\varepsilon,\mu)$ (notably through the factor $(\partial^2_{ss}\widehat G)^{-1}\simeq 1/\varepsilon$ in \eqref{eq:hypergrad}).
We analyze updates of the form
\[
  x_{k+1}=x_k-\eta\,\tilde g_k,
\]
where $\tilde g_k$ is the gradient estimate produced by Algorithm~\ref{alg:ahcco} from minibatch $\mathcal{B}_k$ and inner solution $\tilde s_k$.
We model its deviation from the true gradient by allowing bias:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{E}[\tilde g_k\mid x_k]=\nabla \widehat F_\mu(x_k)+b_k,
  \qquad
  \mathbb{E}\!\left[\|\tilde g_k-\mathbb{E}[\tilde g_k\mid x_k]\|^2\mid x_k\right]\le \sigma^2.\label{eq:stoch-bias-var}
\end{equation}
Here $b_k$ captures both Monte Carlo effects (finite $M$) and inner inexactness (finite $T$).
Lemma~\ref{lem:inner-accuracy} quantifies the inner contribution via $\epsilon'$.

\begin{lemma}[Gradient approximation error]\label{lem:grad-approx}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:stoch}, there exist constants $C_{\mathrm{bias}},C_{\mathrm{var}}>0$ depending on $(L_f,G_g,H_g,\theta,\varepsilon,\mu,\delta)$ such that the gradient estimate $\tilde g_k$ produced by Algorithm~\ref{alg:ahcco} satisfies \eqref{eq:stoch-bias-var} with
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{E}\!\left[\|b_k\|^2\mid x_k\right]\le \frac{C_{\mathrm{bias}}}{M}+C_{\mathrm{bias}}(\epsilon')^2,
  \qquad
  \sigma^2\le \frac{C_{\mathrm{var}}}{M}.\label{eq:bias-var-explicit}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\emph{Discussion in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.}

\paragraph{Preview of the next result.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:sgd} turns the bias/variance model \eqref{eq:stoch-bias-var} and the explicit bounds \eqref{eq:bias-var-explicit} into a standard stationarity guarantee for minimizing the smooth surrogate $\widehat F_\mu$.
The bound decomposes into an optimization term that decays as $O(1/(\eta K))$, a stochastic term $O(\eta/M)$ from minibatching, and an error floor governed by the bias level $O(1/M+(\epsilon')^2)$ induced by finite minibatches and inexact inner solves.

\begin{theorem}[SGD with minibatches and inexact inner solves]\label{thm:sgd}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:stoch}, $\widehat F_\mu$ has $L$-Lipschitz gradient on $\Xc$ for some $L>0$.
Assume $\tilde g_k$ satisfies \eqref{eq:stoch-bias-var} and the explicit bounds \eqref{eq:bias-var-explicit}.
Consider the SGD recursion $x_{k+1}=x_k-\eta \tilde g_k$ with constant step size $\eta\le 1/(2L)$.
Then for any $K\ge 1$,
\begin{align}
  &\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\|\nabla \widehat F_\mu(x_k)\|^2\right]\nonumber\\
  &\;\le\;
  \frac{4(\widehat F_\mu(x_0)-\widehat F_\mu^{\inf})}{\eta K}
  + \frac{2L\eta\,C_{\mathrm{var}}}{M}
  + 4C_{\mathrm{bias}}\!\left(\frac{1}{M}+(\epsilon')^2\right).\label{eq:sgd-rate}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}
\emph{Proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.}

\paragraph{Remarks.}
If the bias terms vanish (e.g., by increasing $M$ and tightening the inner tolerance so that $\tau_k\to 0$), Theorem~\ref{thm:sgd} recovers the usual $O(1/\sqrt{K})$ stationarity rate for the smooth surrogate.
If $\|b_k\|$ does not vanish, the iterates converge to a neighborhood whose size is controlled by the bias level, which is the standard behavior in hypergradient methods with inexact inner solves.
The extension to projected updates $x_{k+1}=\Pi_{\Xc}(x_k-\eta \tilde g_k)$ is standard and can be handled by replacing $\|\nabla \widehat F_\mu(x_k)\|$ with an appropriate gradient mapping.

\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}
We evaluate three questions: (i) whether the proposed bilevel hypergradient method improves the objective--reliability trade-off on controlled CCO benchmarks, (ii) how performance changes with dimension, sample size, and safety level, and (iii) whether the same formulation transfers to a practical data-driven robustness task.

\subsection{Synthetic benchmarks: setup}
All methods solve
\[
  \min_x\ f(x)\quad\text{s.t.}\quad \mathbb{P}\!\left(g(x,Z)\le 0\right)\ge 1-\delta,
\]
where $Z$ is random and $\delta\in(0,1)$ is the target miscoverage.

\paragraph{Problem A: Univariate exponential.}
Decision variable $x\in\mathbb{R}$ and $Z\sim\mathrm{Exp}(\text{mean}=3)$.
We use
\[
  f(x)=x^3 e^x,
  \qquad
  g(x,z)=50\,z\,e^x-5.
\]
The chance constraint $g(x,Z)\le 0$ is equivalent to $Z \le 0.1e^{-x}$, which couples feasibility to the tail of an exponential distribution.

\paragraph{Problem B: Gaussian linear (high-dimensional).}
Decision variable $x\in\mathbb{R}^d$ and $Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_d)$.
We use the quadratic objective
\[
  f(x)=\|x-2\mathbf{1}\|_2^2,
  \qquad
  g(x,z)=x^\top z-1.
\]
Thus the constraint requires $\mathbb{P}(x^\top Z\le 1)\ge 1-\delta$.

\paragraph{Compared methods.}
We compare \textbf{AH-CCO} (ours) against \textbf{TACO} \citep{laguelmalickackooij2021bilevel}, a \textbf{CVaR-constraint} surrogate optimized by penalty \citep{rockafellaruryasev2000cvar}, solver-based conformal encodings \textbf{CPP-MIP}/\textbf{CPP-KKT}/\textbf{SA}/\textbf{SAA} \citep{zhao2024conformal}, and \textbf{KKTBL}.

\paragraph{Protocol and metrics.}
For each case and seed, all methods use the same training samples $\{Z_i\}_{i=1}^M$.
Reliability is evaluated on an independent test set $\{\widetilde Z_j\}_{j=1}^{N_{\mathrm{eval}}}$ with $N_{\mathrm{eval}}=5000$.
We report reliability, runtime, and an $H$-score that combines objective value and feasibility (defined below).
Reliability closeness to the target is summarized by
\begin{equation}
  F_{\mathrm{EC}}(\mathrm{EC}, 1-\delta)
  \;=\;
  \frac{2\min\{\mathrm{EC}, 1-\delta\}}{\mathrm{EC} + (1-\delta)}\in[0,1],\label{eq:fscore}
\end{equation}
which equals $1$ iff $\mathrm{EC}=1-\delta$.
We write $\mathrm{EC}(x)$ for the empirical coverage of a candidate solution $x$ on the evaluation set.
To summarize \emph{optimality and feasibility simultaneously}, we report an $H$-score inspired by the harmonic-mean $F$-measure \citep{vanrijsbergen1979information}.
For each test case, let $f_{\mathrm{best}}$ be the best (lowest) objective value among all methods that return a solution (excluding timeouts/failures).
We define a normalized optimality score
\begin{equation}
  S_{\mathrm{opt}}(x)
  \;:=\;
  \frac{1}{1 + \frac{\max\{0,f(x)-f_{\mathrm{best}}\}}{|f_{\mathrm{best}}|+1}}
  \in(0,1],\label{eq:sopt}
\end{equation}
and combine it with reliability via the harmonic mean
\begin{equation}
  H(x)
  \;:=\;
  \frac{2\,S_{\mathrm{opt}}(x)\,F_{\mathrm{EC}}(\mathrm{EC}(x),1-\delta)}{S_{\mathrm{opt}}(x)+F_{\mathrm{EC}}(\mathrm{EC}(x),1-\delta)}\in[0,1].\label{eq:hscore}
\end{equation}
All synthetic table values are means over two seeds.
Solver-based baselines use a per-instance time limit of $100$s; ``timeout'' indicates at least one run hit the time limit (in which case no averaged solution is reported).

\paragraph{Scaling axes.}
Our main synthetic study is on Problem~B with three sweeps:
\emph{dimension} ($d\in\{10,30,100\}$, fixed $M{=}100$, $\delta{=}0.05$),
\emph{sample size} ($M\in\{100,250,500\}$, fixed $d{=}30$, $\delta{=}0.05$),
and \emph{safety level} ($\delta\in\{0.01,0.05,0.10\}$, fixed $d{=}30$, $M{=}100$).
Problem~A is used as a sanity-check benchmark and reported separately in Table~\ref{tab:cpp-problem}.

\subsection{Data-driven robustness: ImageNet-100 under random corruptions}\label{sec:imagenet100c}
We next evaluate a data-driven chance-constrained learning task motivated by corruption robustness.
We use ImageNet-100, a 100-class subset of ImageNet \citep{deng2009imagenet}, and generate corrupted inputs using common corruption operators from ImageNet-C \citep{hendrycks2019benchmarking}.
The goal is to maintain clean accuracy while controlling failure probability under random corruptions.

\paragraph{Chance-constrained formulation.}
Given a classifier $\ell_\theta(\cdot)\in\mathbb{R}^C$, we define the logit margin
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  m_\theta(\widetilde x,y)
  &:= \ell_\theta(\widetilde x)_y
  - \max_{c\neq y}\ell_\theta(\widetilde x)_c,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
and violation
\begin{equation}
  g(\theta; x,y,\xi)
  \;:=\;
  \kappa - m_\theta(\mathrm{corrupt}(x,\xi),y),
  \label{eq:imagenet-violation}
\end{equation}
so that $g\le 0$ indicates a correct prediction with margin at least $\kappa$.
We group corruptions into $r=4$ families (\textsc{Noise}, \textsc{Blur}, \textsc{Weather}, \textsc{Digital}) and impose one conditional chance constraint per family:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \mathbb{P}\!\left(
    g(\theta;x,y,\xi)\le 0 \,\middle|\, \xi\in\text{family } j
  \right)
  &\ge 1-\delta,\\
  &\qquad j\in\{1,\dots,r\},
  \label{eq:imagenet-family-cco}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where probability is over the joint draw of $(x,y)$ and corruption randomness $\xi$ within family $j$.

\paragraph{Protocol and baselines.}
All methods use the same data split, training corruption sampling procedure, and backbone (ResNet-50).
AH-CCO optimizes a differentiable surrogate of \eqref{eq:imagenet-family-cco} with one scalar inner solve per family and implicit hypergradients for the outer update.
For stability, AH-CCO is warm-started from an ERM checkpoint and then fine-tuned under the chance constraints.
We compare against practical robustness baselines: \textbf{ERM} \citep{goodfellow2016deep}, \textbf{AugMix} \citep{hendrycks2020augmix}, \textbf{TRADES} \citep{zhang2019theoretically}, \textbf{CVaR} \citep{rockafellaruryasev2000cvar}, and \textbf{GroupDRO} \citep{sagawa2020distributionally}.

\paragraph{Metrics and results.}
Table~\ref{tab:imagenet100-main} reports clean Top-1, mean/min corrupted Top-1 across the four families, and mean/min reliability under \eqref{eq:imagenet-family-cco}.
Reliability is summarized with $F_{\mathrm{EC}}$ in \eqref{eq:fscore} and reported as percentages ($100\times F_{\mathrm{EC}}$).
Figure~\ref{fig:imagenet100-acc} visualizes the clean vs.\ mean-corrupted trade-off, while Figure~\ref{fig:imagenet100-fec} breaks down per-family reliability.
In this pilot setting, AH-CCO achieves the strongest joint profile: it improves both clean and corrupted accuracy while also attaining the highest mean/min reliability.
AugMix improves corruption accuracy over ERM but yields weaker reliability, and GroupDRO improves worst-family accuracy but does not match the chance-constraint target as closely (Figure~\ref{fig:imagenet100-fec}).

\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}
\paragraph{When AH-CCO is attractive.}
AH-CCO is designed for settings with a high-dimensional decision variable $x$ and a small number of chance constraints, where the inner quantile computation can be reduced to a one-dimensional solve and the outer step can be handled by scalable first-order methods.
This makes it complementary to solver-based chance-constraint encodings, which can provide strong statistical guarantees but may struggle to scale with dimension and sample size.

\paragraph{Sensitivity and practical guidance.}
The method has three main stability--accuracy knobs: the hinge smoothing $\theta$, the strong convexity parameter $\varepsilon$, and the penalty parameter $\mu$.
Smaller $\theta$ and $\varepsilon$ reduce approximation bias (Theorem~\ref{thm:quantile-approx}), while too small $\varepsilon$ can amplify hypergradients through the factor $(\partial^2_{ss}\widehat G)^{-1}\simeq 1/\varepsilon$ in \eqref{eq:hypergrad}.
In practice we therefore use moderate $\varepsilon$ and gradient clipping on larger instances, and we decrease $\mu$ over iterations to progressively tighten feasibility.
The inner solve is robust because it is one-dimensional and can be bracketed deterministically (Algorithm~\ref{alg:inner-bisect}); increasing the minibatch size $M$ and tightening the inner tolerance improves reliability of the hypergradient estimate.

\paragraph{Limitations of the current theory.}
Our guarantees are for optimization of a \emph{penalized, smoothed surrogate} objective $\widehat F_\mu$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:sgd} establishes convergence to an approximate stationary point of $\widehat F_\mu$ under minibatching and inexact inner solves, but it does not imply global optimality, nor does it certify exact feasibility for the original chance constraint.
Moreover, our convergence result is stated for a fixed penalty parameter $\mu$; a decreasing schedule $\{\mu_k\}$ is a practical homotopy heuristic that tightens feasibility but is not directly covered by Theorem~\ref{thm:sgd}.
Finally, the approximation and stability bounds rely on local regularity around the target quantile (Assumption~\ref{ass:quantile-regularity}); for heavy-tailed or irregular distributions, the density lower bound may fail and quantile sensitivity can degrade.

\paragraph{Beyond the current scope.}
Extensions to multiple (or joint) chance constraints are conceptually straightforward but introduce additional statistical and computational challenges, especially when constraints interact or when the number of constraints is large.
Bridging the remaining gap between stationary points of surrogates and end-to-end guarantees for the original CCO objective under realistic model classes is an important direction for future work.

The outer loop follows Algorithm~\ref{alg:ahcco}.
The inner scalar variable is solved by warm-started bisection (Algorithm~\ref{alg:inner-bisect}) on the monotone optimality condition \eqref{eq:phi-plugin}.

\paragraph{Baselines and time limits.}
We include TACO \citep{laguelmalickackooij2021bilevel}, a CVaR-penalty surrogate \citep{rockafellaruryasev2000cvar}, and solver-based conformal encodings (CPP-MIP/CPP-KKT/SA/SAA) \citep{zhao2024conformal}, as well as KKTBL.

\paragraph{Dataset and model.}
We use ImageNet-100 (a 100-class subset of ImageNet \citep{deng2009imagenet}) with standard train/val splits and a ResNet-50 backbone.
Images are resized/cropped to $224\times 224$ and trained with batch size $128$ for $10$ epochs using SGD (learning rate $0.1$, momentum $0.9$, weight decay $10^{-4}$).

\paragraph{Corruptions and families.}
We follow the ImageNet-C robustness motivation \citep{hendrycks2019benchmarking} and use four corruption families (\textsc{Noise}, \textsc{Blur}, \textsc{Weather}, \textsc{Digital}) with severities sampled uniformly from $\{1,\dots,5\}$.
During training and evaluation, corruptions are applied on-the-fly by sampling severities independently per batch.

\paragraph{Chance constraints and AH-CCO hyperparameters.}
We impose one chance constraint per family at target level $1-\delta$ with $\delta=0.05$ and margin threshold $\kappa=0$ (Section~\ref{sec:imagenet100c}).
For AH-CCO we use smoothing $\theta=0.2$, strong convexity $\varepsilon=0.005$, penalty parameter $\mu=0.1$, and $40$ bisection steps in the inner solve.
To stabilize training, AH-CCO is warm-started from an ERM checkpoint and the proxy term is ramped in over the first 5 epochs.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
We compare against ERM \citep{goodfellow2016deep}, AugMix \citep{hendrycks2020augmix} (mixture width 3, severity 3, JSD weight 6), TRADES \citep{zhang2019theoretically} ($\ell_\infty$ perturbations with $\epsilon{=}0.0078$, 3 steps, $\beta{=}3$), CVaR \citep{rockafellaruryasev2000cvar} (tail level $\alpha{=}0.1$), and GroupDRO \citep{sagawa2020distributionally} over the four corruption families.

\paragraph{Evaluation.}
Clean Top-1 is computed on the validation set.
For each family, corrupted Top-1 and empirical coverage are computed by re-corrupting validation images with random severities and measuring whether the logit margin exceeds $\kappa$; we then report mean/min across families.

\section{Proofs}\label{app:proofs}

\paragraph{Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:unique-sstar}.}
\begin{proof}
Fix $x$ and define $Y:=g(x,Z)$.
For fixed $x$, the function $s\mapsto G_\delta(x,s)= s + \frac{1}{\delta}\mathbb{E}[(Y-s)_+]$ is convex.
Standard subgradient calculus for the positive-part function yields
\[
  \partial_s G_\delta(x,s)
  \;=\;
  \left[\,1-\frac{1}{\delta}\mathbb{P}(Y\ge s)\;,\;1-\frac{1}{\delta}\mathbb{P}(Y> s)\,\right].
\]
Thus $0\in \partial_s G_\delta(x,s)$ if and only if $\mathbb{P}(Y>s)\le \delta \le \mathbb{P}(Y\ge s)$, i.e., $s$ belongs to the $(1-\delta)$-quantile set of $Y$.
Because $Y$ has a continuous distribution, $\mathbb{P}(Y=s)=0$ for all $s$ and the optimality condition reduces to $\mathbb{P}(Y>s)=\delta$.
Let $q:=\Q_{1-\delta}(Y)$.
Continuity of the CDF implies $\mathbb{P}(Y>q)=\delta$, hence $q$ is a minimizer.
Finally, since the CDF is strictly increasing at $q$, the tail probability $s\mapsto \mathbb{P}(Y>s)$ crosses the level $\delta$ at a unique point, so $\arg\min_s G_\delta(x,s)=\{q\}$ and the minimizer is unique.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Auxiliary lemmas for Theorem~\ref{thm:quantile-approx}.}
\begin{lemma}[Derivative bounds for the hinge smoothing]\label{lem:h-deriv-bounds}
The smoothing $h_\theta$ in \eqref{eq:htheta} satisfies $0\le h'_\theta(u)\le 1$ for all $u$ and $h''_\theta(u)=0$ for $|u|>\theta$.
Moreover, for $|u|\le \theta/2$ we have $h''_\theta(u)\ge \frac{9}{16\theta}$.
\end{lemma}

\paragraph{Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:h-deriv-bounds}.}
\begin{proof}
By construction $h_\theta$ coincides with $\max\{u,0\}$ outside $[-\theta,\theta]$, hence $h'_\theta\in\{0,1\}$ and $h''_\theta=0$ there.
Inside $[-\theta,\theta]$, $h_\theta$ is a quartic polynomial whose first derivative interpolates between $0$ and $1$ and whose second derivative is
$h''_\theta(u)=\frac{3}{4\theta}\left(1-\frac{u^2}{\theta^2}\right)$.
For $|u|\le \theta/2$, $1-u^2/\theta^2\ge 3/4$, giving $h''_\theta(u)\ge 9/(16\theta)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Smoothing-only bias]\label{lem:smoothing-bias}
Assume \eqref{eq:density-lower} holds.
Let $s_\theta$ be any solution to $\mathbb{E}[h'_\theta(Y-s_\theta)]=\delta$ (equivalently, any minimizer of $\widehat G_{\delta,\theta,0}(x,\cdot)$).
Then the true quantile satisfies $q\in[s_\theta-\theta,s_\theta+\theta]$, hence $|s_\theta-q|\le \theta$.
\end{lemma}

\paragraph{Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:smoothing-bias}.}
\begin{proof}
Let $F_Y$ be the CDF of $Y$.
Since $0\le h'_\theta\le 1$ and $h'_\theta(u)=1$ for $u\ge \theta$ and $h'_\theta(u)=0$ for $u\le -\theta$, we have
\[
  \mathbf{1}\{Y\ge s+\theta\}\;\le\; h'_\theta(Y-s)\;\le\;\mathbf{1}\{Y\ge s-\theta\}
  \qquad\text{a.s.}
\]
Taking expectations and setting $\mathbb{E}[h'_\theta(Y-s_\theta)]=\delta$ yields
$\mathbb{P}(Y\ge s_\theta+\theta)\le \delta\le \mathbb{P}(Y\ge s_\theta-\theta)$, i.e.,
$F_Y(s_\theta-\theta)\le 1-\delta\le F_Y(s_\theta+\theta)$.
The right inequality implies $q\le s_\theta+\theta$.
For the left inequality, suppose for contradiction that $s_\theta-\theta>q$.
Then $s_\theta-\theta\ge q$ and by \eqref{eq:density-lower} we have
$F_Y(s_\theta-\theta)\ge F_Y(q) + \int_q^{\min\{s_\theta-\theta,q+r\}} p_Y(t)\,dt > 1-\delta$,
contradicting $F_Y(s_\theta-\theta)\le 1-\delta$.
Hence $s_\theta-\theta\le q$, and therefore $q\in[s_\theta-\theta,s_\theta+\theta]$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:quantile-approx}.}
\begin{proof}
Let $H_\theta(s):=\mathbb{E}[h'_\theta(Y-s)]$, which is continuous and nonincreasing in $s$.
Define the function
\[
  F(s):=H_\theta(s)-\delta(1+\varepsilon s).
\]
By \eqref{eq:Htheta-root}, $\widehat s^\star$ satisfies $F(\widehat s^\star)=0$.
Moreover, since $H_\theta$ is nonincreasing and $s\mapsto -\delta\varepsilon s$ is strictly decreasing, $F$ is strictly decreasing, hence it has at most one root.

Let $s_\theta$ be any solution to $H_\theta(s_\theta)=\delta$; by Lemma~\ref{lem:smoothing-bias}, $|s_\theta-q|\le \theta$.

\textbf{Step 1 (localization).}
We show $\widehat s^\star\in[q-r/2,q+r/2]$ under \eqref{eq:eps-localize}.
Using the pointwise bounds from the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:smoothing-bias},
\[
  \mathbf{1}\{Y\ge s+\theta\}\;\le\; h'_\theta(Y-s)\;\le\;\mathbf{1}\{Y\ge s-\theta\}
  \qquad\text{a.s.},
\]
we have $ \mathbb{P}(Y\ge s+\theta)\le H_\theta(s)\le \mathbb{P}(Y\ge s-\theta)$.
At the right endpoint $s_+:=q+r/2$, this yields
\[
  H_\theta(s_+)\le \mathbb{P}\!\left(Y\ge q+\frac{r}{2}-\theta\right)
  = 1-F_Y\!\left(q+\frac{r}{2}-\theta\right)
  \le \delta - m\left(\frac{r}{2}-\theta\right),
 \]
where the last inequality uses $F_Y(q)=1-\delta$ and \eqref{eq:density-lower}.
Since $|s_+|\le |q|+r/2$, we have
\[
  \delta(1+\varepsilon s_+)\ge \delta\left(1-\varepsilon\left(|q|+\frac{r}{2}\right)\right).
\]
Combining the two displays and using \eqref{eq:eps-localize} gives $F(s_+)<0$.
Similarly, at the left endpoint $s_-:=q-r/2$ we have
\[
  H_\theta(s_-)\ge \mathbb{P}\!\left(Y\ge q-\frac{r}{2}+\theta\right)
  = 1-F_Y\!\left(q-\frac{r}{2}+\theta\right)
  \ge \delta + m\left(\frac{r}{2}-\theta\right),
\]
and $\delta(1+\varepsilon s_-)\le \delta\left(1+\varepsilon\left(|q|+\frac{r}{2}\right)\right)$, so \eqref{eq:eps-localize} implies $F(s_-)>0$.
Because $F$ is continuous and strictly decreasing, it has a unique root in $(s_-,s_+)$.
Thus $\widehat s^\star\in[q-r/2,q+r/2]$.

\textbf{Step 2 (regularization bias).}
The first-order optimality condition for $\widehat s^\star$ is \eqref{eq:Htheta-root}.
Subtracting $H_\theta(s_\theta)=\delta$ from \eqref{eq:Htheta-root} gives
$H_\theta(\widehat s^\star)-H_\theta(s_\theta)=\delta\varepsilon \widehat s^\star$.
By the mean value theorem, there exists $\xi$ between $\widehat s^\star$ and $s_\theta$ such that
\[
  H_\theta(\widehat s^\star)-H_\theta(s_\theta)=H_\theta'(\xi)\,(\widehat s^\star-s_\theta),
  \qquad
  H_\theta'(\xi)=-\mathbb{E}\!\left[h''_\theta(Y-\xi)\right].
\]
Since $\widehat s^\star\in[q-r/2,q+r/2]$ and $|s_\theta-q|\le \theta$ with $\theta\le r/2$, we have $|\,\xi-q\,|\le r/2$.
Therefore $[\xi-\theta/2,\xi+\theta/2]\subseteq[q-r,q+r]$, where $p_Y\ge m$.
Using Lemma~\ref{lem:h-deriv-bounds} and \eqref{eq:density-lower},
\[
  \mathbb{E}[h''_\theta(Y-\xi)]
  \;\ge\;
  \frac{9}{16\theta}\,\mathbb{P}\!\left(|Y-\xi|\le \frac{\theta}{2}\right)
  \;\ge\;
  \frac{9}{16\theta}\int_{\xi-\theta/2}^{\xi+\theta/2} p_Y(t)\,dt
  \;\ge\;
  \frac{9}{16\theta}\cdot m\theta
  \;=\;\frac{9m}{16}.
\]
Hence $|H_\theta'(\xi)|\ge 9m/16$, and therefore
\[
  |\widehat s^\star-s_\theta|
  \;=\;
  \frac{\delta\varepsilon|\widehat s^\star|}{|H_\theta'(\xi)|}
  \;\le\;
  \frac{16\delta}{9m}\left(|q|+\frac{r}{2}\right)\varepsilon,
\]
where we used $\widehat s^\star\in[q-r/2,q+r/2]$ in the last inequality.
Combining with $|s_\theta-q|\le \theta$ yields \eqref{eq:shat-q-bound}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:inner-accuracy}.}
\begin{proof}
Fix an iteration $k$ and minibatch $\mathcal{B}_k=\{z_k^{(j)}\}_{j=1}^M$.
Let $\widehat G_k(s):=\widehat G_{\delta,\theta,\varepsilon}(x_k,s;\mathcal{B}_k)$ and let $\widehat s^\star_k:=\widehat s^\star(x_k;\mathcal{B}_k)$ be its unique minimizer.
The first-order condition is $\partial_s \widehat G_k(\widehat s^\star_k)=0$.

\textbf{Bisection rate.}
Because $0\le h'_\theta\le 1$, we have for any $s$
\[
  0\le \frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^M h'_\theta\!\left(g(x_k,z_k^{(j)})-s\right)\le 1.
\]
Therefore, for $s^{\mathrm{L}}=-1/\varepsilon$,
\[
  \partial_s \widehat G_k(s^{\mathrm{L}})
  = 1-\frac{1}{\delta M}\sum_{j=1}^M h'_\theta(g(x_k,z_k^{(j)})-s^{\mathrm{L}})+\varepsilon s^{\mathrm{L}}
  \le 1 + \varepsilon\left(-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)=0,
\]
and for $s^{\mathrm{U}}=1/(\delta\varepsilon)$,
\[
  \partial_s \widehat G_k(s^{\mathrm{U}})
  \ge 1-\frac{1}{\delta}\cdot 1 + \varepsilon\left(\frac{1}{\delta\varepsilon}\right)=1.
\]
Since $s\mapsto \widehat G_k(s)$ is $\varepsilon$-strongly convex, $\partial_s \widehat G_k$ is strictly increasing, hence the unique root $\widehat s^\star_k$ lies in $[s^{\mathrm{L}},s^{\mathrm{U}}]$.
After $T$ bisection steps on this bracket, the returned $\tilde s_k$ satisfies
$
|\tilde s_k-\widehat s^\star_k|\le (s^{\mathrm{U}}-s^{\mathrm{L}})2^{-T},
$
which is \eqref{eq:inner-bisect-rate}.

\textbf{Statistical error.}
Let $\widehat s^\star(x_k)$ be the population minimizer in \eqref{eq:shat-star}, and write $\phi(s):=\partial_s\widehat G_{\delta,\theta,\varepsilon}(x_k,s)$ and
$\phi_k(s):=\partial_s\widehat G_{\delta,\theta,\varepsilon}(x_k,s;\mathcal{B}_k)$.
Both $\phi$ and $\phi_k$ are increasing with derivative at least $\varepsilon$ (since $\partial^2_{ss}\widehat G\ge \varepsilon$).
Thus,
\[
  |\widehat s^\star_k-\widehat s^\star(x_k)|
  \le \frac{|\phi_k(\widehat s^\star(x_k))-\phi(\widehat s^\star(x_k))|}{\varepsilon}.
\]
Using $\phi(\widehat s^\star(x_k))=0$ and the fact that $\phi_k$ replaces an expectation of a $[0,1]$-valued random variable by its sample average, we obtain
\[
  \mathbb{E}\!\left[|\widehat s^\star_k-\widehat s^\star(x_k)|\mid x_k\right]
  \le \frac{1}{\delta\varepsilon}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left|\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^M W_j-\mathbb{E}[W_1]\right|\right]
  \le \frac{1}{\delta\varepsilon}\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{Var}(W_1)}{M}}
  \le \frac{1}{2\delta\varepsilon\sqrt{M}},
\]
where $W_j:=h'_\theta(g(x_k,Z_j)-\widehat s^\star(x_k))\in[0,1]$ and we used $\mathrm{Var}(W_1)\le 1/4$.
Combining with \eqref{eq:inner-bisect-rate} and the triangle inequality yields \eqref{eq:inner-eps-s}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Discussion of Lemma~\ref{lem:grad-approx}.}
Lemma~\ref{lem:grad-approx} can be justified by combining (i) stability of the implicit map $x\mapsto \widehat s^\star(x)$ under strong convexity and (ii) concentration of minibatch averages.
Concretely, the hypergradient \eqref{eq:hypergrad} depends on ratios of the form
$
\partial^2_{xs}\widehat G/\partial^2_{ss}\widehat G
$
evaluated at $(x_k,\widehat s^\star(x_k))$, while Algorithm~\ref{alg:ahcco} uses the sample-average analog evaluated at $(x_k,\tilde s_k)$.
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:stoch} and Lemma~\ref{lem:h-deriv-bounds}, the relevant sample-average quantities are averages of bounded random variables (involving $h'_\theta$ and $h''_\theta$), and the denominator is bounded away from zero by $\varepsilon$.
Thus, deviations due to finite minibatches scale as $O(M^{-1/2})$, while the evaluation error due to the inexact inner solve scales as $O(|\tilde s_k-\widehat s^\star(x_k)|)$, which is controlled by $\epsilon'$ via Lemma~\ref{lem:inner-accuracy}.
Squaring and taking expectations yields a bound of the form \eqref{eq:bias-var-explicit} with constants depending on $(G_g,H_g,\theta,\varepsilon,\mu,\delta)$.

\paragraph{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:sgd}.}
\begin{proof}
By $L$-smoothness of $\widehat F_\mu$, for any update direction $d$ we have
\[
  \widehat F_\mu(x_k+d)\le \widehat F_\mu(x_k) + \langle \nabla \widehat F_\mu(x_k), d\rangle + \frac{L}{2}\|d\|^2.
\]
With $d=-\eta \tilde g_k$, taking conditional expectation given $x_k$ and using \eqref{eq:stoch-bias-var} gives
\begin{align*}
  \mathbb{E}\!\left[\widehat F_\mu(x_{k+1})\mid x_k\right]
  &\le \widehat F_\mu(x_k) - \eta \left\langle \nabla \widehat F_\mu(x_k), \mathbb{E}[\tilde g_k\mid x_k]\right\rangle
  + \frac{L\eta^2}{2}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\|\tilde g_k\|^2\mid x_k\right]\\
  &= \widehat F_\mu(x_k) - \eta \|\nabla \widehat F_\mu(x_k)\|^2 - \eta\langle \nabla \widehat F_\mu(x_k), b_k\rangle
  + \frac{L\eta^2}{2}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\|\tilde g_k\|^2\mid x_k\right].
\end{align*}
Using $-\langle a,b\rangle\le \frac{1}{2}\|a\|^2+\frac{1}{2}\|b\|^2$ and the bound
\[
  \mathbb{E}\!\left[\|\tilde g_k\|^2\mid x_k\right]
  \le 2\|\nabla \widehat F_\mu(x_k)\|^2 + 2\|b_k\|^2 + \sigma^2,
\]
we obtain
\[
  \mathbb{E}\!\left[\widehat F_\mu(x_{k+1})\mid x_k\right]
  \le \widehat F_\mu(x_k)
  -\left(\frac{\eta}{2}-L\eta^2\right)\|\nabla \widehat F_\mu(x_k)\|^2
  +\left(\frac{\eta}{2}+L\eta^2\right)\|b_k\|^2
  +\frac{L\eta^2}{2}\sigma^2.
\]
If $\eta\le 1/(2L)$, then $\frac{\eta}{2}-L\eta^2\ge \frac{\eta}{4}$ and $\frac{\eta}{2}+L\eta^2\le \eta$, so
\[
  \mathbb{E}\!\left[\widehat F_\mu(x_{k+1})\mid x_k\right]
  \le \widehat F_\mu(x_k)
  -\frac{\eta}{4}\|\nabla \widehat F_\mu(x_k)\|^2
  +\eta\|b_k\|^2
  +\frac{L\eta^2}{2}\sigma^2.
\]
Taking total expectation and summing over $k=0,\dots,K-1$ gives
\[
  \frac{\eta}{4}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\|\nabla \widehat F_\mu(x_k)\|^2\right]
  \le \widehat F_\mu(x_0)-\mathbb{E}[\widehat F_\mu(x_K)] + \eta\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\|b_k\|^2\right] + \frac{L\eta^2K}{2}\sigma^2
  \le \widehat F_\mu(x_0)-\widehat F_\mu^{\inf} + \eta\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\|b_k\|^2\right] + \frac{L\eta^2K}{2}\sigma^2,
\]
which rearranges to
\[
  \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\|\nabla \widehat F_\mu(x_k)\|^2\right]
  \;\le\;
  \frac{4(\widehat F_\mu(x_0)-\widehat F_\mu^{\inf})}{\eta K}
  + 2L\eta\sigma^2
  + \frac{4}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\|b_k\|^2\right].
\]
Using $\sigma^2\le C_{\mathrm{var}}/M$ and $\mathbb{E}[\|b_k\|^2]\le C_{\mathrm{bias}}(1/M+(\epsilon')^2)$ from \eqref{eq:bias-var-explicit} for all $k$ yields \eqref{eq:sgd-rate}.
\end{proof}
